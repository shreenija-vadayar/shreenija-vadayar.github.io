overview: "A wearable installation featuring seven mechanical lotus-inspired flowers that respond to nearby visitors through movement and light. It was presented at the 2025 CU Boulder Design Expo and won First Prize in the Autodesk Make it Wearable Engineering Student Contest."
problem: "The project explores how human presence, connection, and support can help people through adversity, using an interactive wearable form as the storyteller."
role:
  - "I independently developed the concept, garment, mechanical flower designs, fabrication, electronics integration, and programming."
  - "I developed the visual concept through moodboards, sketches, renderings, and 3D models, drawing inspiration from designers including Iris van Herpen and Yin Gao."
  - "I iterated on the flower mechanisms and their mounting within the garment and mannequin, then tested and troubleshot the integrated system. Challenges included insufficient stem rigidity, fitting servos within the mannequin and garment assembly, loose connections, and powering multiple flowers."
technical:
  - "Modeling and fabrication: SketchUp, 3D printing, brass rods and mechanical structures"
  - "Motion and sensing: micro servos, VL53L0X distance sensors"
  - "Electronics: Adafruit Trinket M0 boards, NeoPixel LEDs, soldering and wiring"
  - "Software: Arduino IDE"
  - "Garment: sewing and garment construction"
outcome:
  - "I presented the installation at the 2025 CU Boulder Design Expo and engaged directly with more than 25 visitors."
  - "The lighting worked and all seven flowers functioned to some degree, but three achieved the full intended blooming and wilting motion."
  - "Next steps would be stronger stems with more flexibility, more reliable mounting and connections, and improved power distribution."
